## 1. The story in one paragraph

Two colleagues went away, learned four things, and came back to a room that is going to be asked to decide. The spine is a Resonate sparkline with three swings. Swing one: enterprise is spending hard and stumbling wherever nobody drew a line (what is), and the four lessons are the way through (what could be). Swing two, the heart of the deck: on paper the City has no program (what is), yet once you count the work, the recognition, the staff voices and the sandbox, the City already has people, infrastructure and a working fleet (what could be). Swing three: the first steps are only meetings and staff time (what is, deliberately modest), and from them a plan with lanes, skills and measurement (what could be). Then the ask, and the reveal that the plan was the four lessons applied. The audience is the hero: leadership draws the lines, each DCIO decides autonomy, the CISO owns the controls. The single idea to leave behind: **the City is further along than its paperwork says; what is missing is organization and six decisions that are yours to make.** The STAR moment is already in the script but not yet on the stage: slide 33, where the gaps named on slide 13 come back and each one has a decision sitting beside it, waiting for a person.

Sparkline sketch (text):

```
COULD BE      03 lessons      09-12 the lessons taught     15-21 what the City really has     23-29 the plan      34 reveal
               /      \          /              \            /                      \          /         \        /
WHAT IS   01-02 course  04-08 enterprise stumbles   13-14 on paper, no program   22 only meetings   30-33 ready + the ask   35 talk
```

Part 4 is the long climb and the one the DCIOs must feel; Part 6 should land as "your move," not "our plan."

## 2. The visual system

Most of this already exists in the frames. The job is to name it, keep it, and stop the six rebuilt slides from falling out of it.

**Cast (who recurs, how drawn).**
- *The staff figure*: green outline person (09, 11, 15, 23, 24, 32). Green means the City's own people at work. It is the hero's proxy; it is the only thing that climbs, gathers, approves.
- *The agent*: the hexagon with two dots (05, 07, 20, 21). Navy, never green, never a face beyond the two dots.
- *The request card*: a small document (06, 10, 14, 32). Work in motion. Green when it is AI-drafted (32), navy when it is a request.
- *The gate*: the hurdle shape on the stair (12) and on the readiness line (21, 33). This should be the one gate everywhere: slide 10's permission check, slide 7's permission checker frame, 21's checkpoints, 29's expansion decision. Right now 10 uses two bars and 7 uses a box; unify on the hurdle.
- *The round table*: 22, 23, 24, 25. The consortium. A seat at it is the mark of the program team.
- *The fenced yard*: 19, 24. The sandbox. Draw it small whenever the sandbox is named.
- *The department box with a lantern*: 15, 23. The lit grid is the deck's signature image; nothing else in the deck looks like Austin.
- *The four lesson panels with green numerals*: 03, 34. Bookends.

**Geography.** One drawn object carries Part 4 and the ask: a route, flat, with no destination marker and no figure travelling on it. Solid where something exists, dashed where it is still to be established, gates where a checkpoint stands. It appears on 13 (blueprint), 21 (what stands on it), 33 (where each decision would act). I accept the first audit's route in geometry but not in its last sentence: on 33 nothing inks and nothing opens, because those decisions have not been made. Keep slide 03's thin agenda line (three dots) visually distinct from the route: thin single rule with dots versus a double-rule road. Slide 20's double-rule "foundation" currently uses the same stroke as 21's leftover road; give 20 stacked slabs so the road means one thing.

**Colour semantics** (the script already states these; hold the line): green = City people and process; indigo = evidence from the course and the outside world; deep-green tick = a check passed, nothing else; orange = a person deciding or approving, nothing else; grey = problems, blockers, failures, budgets, things not proposed; sky, gold, pink = the four levels on 30 only. One extension to decide as a room: a **hollow orange ring** = a decision that still needs a person. It is the honest way to draw the ask on 33.

**Motion vocabulary (verb = meaning).** *draw* = this exists; *wipe (dashed)* = not yet; *travel* = work moving; *stop* = a limit holds; *open* = a person let it through; *gather* = people join; *tick lands* = a check passed; *mark lands (orange)* = a person decided; *dim/cross* = not proposed. One verb carries each slide. Nothing sweeps, bounces or speeds up. Labels arrive with their objects and stay still while the object acts.

**Composition rules.** Title, green rule, two-sentence band, then one drawing field. Evidence sits in a stationary indigo callout with its source named on screen (slide 06 is the model). Illustrations say so in a slate caption (06's "Illustrative flow, not measured throughput" is the model). Equal sizes on a shared baseline whenever the script says "not a ranking." Six columns is a layout for six separate decisions, never an arrow chain.

**Retire.** The 14-glyph icon rows (compass, cylinder, key, the X-shaped tools glyph that reads as "wrong" on 14 and 28); arrows between items that are not in sequence (33); the bracket and double rule on 21; the scroll on 13; the cylinder standing in for a budget on 13 and 33 (it reads as a database, beside a data-access key no less).

## 4. Risks and what I'd fight for

**Where I expect push-back.**
- *The route.* A designer may say a road implies progress and distance, which the honesty pass removed. My answer: no figure travels, no destination is drawn, segments are equal, and on 33 nothing inks. If the room still prefers a structure metaphor (slide 20's foundation, the yard), I can live with it, but it must be one object on 13, 21 and 33, not three layouts.
- *The hollow orange ring.* It extends the brand's orange rule. If the brand owner says no, the fallback is the six columns with no ring and the title doing the work; weaker, still honest.
- *Script changes.* 33's labels, a "BCG" caption on 04, "Illustrative" captions on 10 and 26. The rule is script first; these are four small They see edits, and I would make them before any world is touched.
- *Carry across worlds.* 13 and 21 appear to share the "stands" world (I inferred that from the frame names; I did not read the world files). 33 is in "ready," so the route must redraw there. That is fine; redrawing is one `draw`.
- *Density on 33.* Three signposts, three gates, six minis, six rings at 1366×768. Minis at about 60 px, labels on two lines, and the road unlabelled apart from its six short names. If it will not fit, drop the gates from 33 and keep only the three signposts; the gates can live on 21 alone.

**The three calls I'd defend hardest.**
1. **One gate shape, everywhere.** Slide 07's checker, 10's permission check, 12's stair, 21's checkpoints, 29's expansion decision, 33's checkpoints. The hurdle is already on 12, 21 and 33. When the City's own readiness is drawn with lesson four's gate, the reveal on 34 has been pre-wired since minute eight, and nobody has to be told.
2. **Nothing on 33 is done.** No inked segment, no opened gate, no tick. The 2026-10-20 fixes removed a catch-up date; letting decisions "unlock" the route would put the same overclaim back in a politer form. Six requests, six empty rings, a plan waiting on people.
3. **Evidence is labelled on the stage, not only in the notes.** 06 does it right (source, "forecast," "illustrative"). 04 should carry "BCG, October 2026"; 26 should say its dots are illustrative; 10 should say it is a design. These DCIOs will repeat what they see, and what they see must be the same thing the speaker is willing to defend.

What I don't know: whether Azure AI Foundry, the production route and the Amazon status are as drawn; whether the quoted staff have agreed to be shown; whether the practitioner and training numbers hold. I kept every label to the script and flagged the four places where the script would need a new line.
