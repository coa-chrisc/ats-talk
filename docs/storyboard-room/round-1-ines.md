## 1. The story in one paragraph

Two of your own people went away, came back with four plain rules, and then did something braver than reporting: they held the rules up against the City and found the City already doing most of it, unorganized and uncounted. The emotional arc is a correction of self-image. Act one is sober (enterprise spent, enterprise stumbled, here is why); act two is the four rules, taught by things stopping at gates and people choosing the smaller tool; act three is the turn, "we have more than the paper says", built from lanterns lighting in department boxes, a wall of plaques, staff quoted in their own words, a fenced yard that already exists; act four is modest and procedural (meet, form a team, listen, sort, deliver, train, measure), which is the point, because modesty is what makes the ask credible; act five hands the pen to the room: leadership draws the lines, each DCIO sets autonomy, the CISO owns the controls, and six decisions sit waiting. The single idea to leave behind: **the work is already here; what is missing is the decision.** Every visual choice should make "already here" tangible and "decision" visibly unmade.

**Geography and metaphor.** The deck has two honest metaphors already in the script's own spine: "foundations" (20, 27, 13's "in place / still to establish") and "the lines leadership draws" (31). I would *not* build a horizontal route for 13, 21, 33. A road with a dashed stretch ahead rereads, from the back row, as a progress bar and invites "how far along are we", exactly the claim the 2026-10-20 pass removed. What already links those three slides, and costs nothing in new words, is the deck's **dashed-outline convention**: dashed means "not yet established" on 13 (three plots), 19 (Amazon), 20 (the design boundary), 22 (the things we are not proposing), 29 (target). Make that the through-line: 13 draws three dashed plots; 21 does not touch them (it is about what *is* solid); 33 brings the same three plots back and shows which decision answers each, with the plots still dashed. Gaps named, gaps answered, nothing claimed.

**Colour semantics (as the frames already use them; write them down and police them).** Green: City people and process at work. Indigo: outside evidence (course, Gartner, BCG, the recognition wall). Deep green: a check passed, ticks only. Orange: a person deciding, the mark only. Grey: not proposed, failed, awaiting (22, 8, 15's tag, 24's empty levels). Dashed: not yet established. Sky, gold, pink: the authority levels on 30 and nowhere else. Two things to fix under this rule: the green angle-marks at the row ends on 20 read as half-ticks (are those passes?), and the plain-tool "T" on 9 is the only solid navy fill in the deck, which makes it look like a logo rather than a tool.

**Motion vocabulary (six verbs; each means one thing).**

- *Draw on* = something is being established (a fence, a table, a plot, a gate).
- *Land* = a thing arrives and is placed; labels land with their object and then hold still.
- *Travel* = work moving toward a decision point. Only the card and the people travel. Constant speed through a path, ease-out only on arrival.
- *Stop / wait* = the card halts at a gate or beside a person. A stop deserves 0.4 to 0.7 s of stillness. This is the most important verb in the deck.
- *Approve / pass* = the orange mark lands; the tick lands. 0.4 s, no overshoot.
- *Light / ink* = a fill rises from wash to active (a lantern, a plot going from dashed to solid). Never on anything that could be read as a measurement.

Retire: fade-in rows of glyph + bold label + caption; arrows between items that are parallel rather than sequential; calendar cells and chairs arriving one at a time; any sweep, speed-up, or bounce.

**Composition rules.** Title top-left, two lines, green rule; story band; one drawing field below, roughly y 380 to 700 at 1366. The subject of the slide must fill the field's height; the six rebuilt slides put their subject at a third of it. Labels at most two lines; a caption under 18 px at 1366 is invisible from the back of a boardroom (33 has twelve lines of about 17 px). If a slide needs more than nine labels, it is two slides or it is a list, and a list should look like one (as 8 honestly does). Left-to-right is time; top-to-bottom is hierarchy; never mix them on one slide (31 is the one legitimate top-down slide).

## 4. Risks and what I would fight for

**Where I expect pushback.**

- *From the script discipline:* my six rebuilds need no new on-screen words; the ledger rules on 10, the rings and callback lines on 33, and the bracket on 29 are all unlabelled strokes. But the *They see* paragraphs for 10, 13, 21, 28, 29 and 33 must be rewritten to describe these beats, and the script rule says script first. Budget that writing before any world is touched.
- *From engineering:* worlds join by fade and carry nothing across, so 13's plots on 33 are a redraw in the `ready` world from a shared layout constant, not a carry; 13 and 21 share the `stands` world and can share a module. Cast-scale miniatures on 33 add parts, and every part must be moved by a step; the count is fine (six cards, six rings, three lines, three plots) but it is more than the twelve shows there now. The one-lap card on 28 and the two-trip card on 10 use `to` along paths, same as 23's walkers; nothing new in the kit.
- *From the honesty reviewer:* 29 now shows a request being approved. It is labelled illustrative and nothing is counted, but someone will ask whether a drawn approval implies a deployed system. The answer is in the tag that lands first and the absence of any number; if that is not enough, keep the trip but drop the orange mark and let the card simply stop beside the staff figure. I would rather keep the mark, because the measure's own words are "staff-approved".
- *From the owner:* retiring the glyph-row grammar touches six slides and the temptation will be to fix the four visible defects (collisions on 13, the X on 14 and 28, the arrows on 33) and stop. The defects are symptoms; the rows are the disease.

**The three calls I would defend hardest.**

1. **Subjects at cast scale, and only the cast moves.** A boardroom screen is read from six metres. A 40 px glyph with a 17 px caption is a handout, not a slide. Every one of the six slides should put its subject at the scale of 9's figure or 23's table, and the thing that moves should be a character the audience already knows.
2. **No route for 13, 21, 33; the dashed plots instead.** The route is the more cinematic idea and I understand its appeal, but a road reads as progress, and this deck earned its credibility by refusing to claim progress it cannot measure. Dashed-to-answered is the same payoff ("the gaps we named are the decisions we ask for") with no odometer.
3. **33 ends on six empty rings.** The last content slide before the bookend should leave the room holding something unfinished that only they can finish. Arrows, an underline, or filled marks would close it for them. Leave it open, hold the frame, and let Chris and Stefan ask the question.
