## The eight disputes

**1. The through-line: 13 → 21 → 33.** Mara's map. The policy inked as a solid green frame around the department grid is the single best drawing idea in the three memos: it says what an acceptable-use policy literally is, and it says "the City is a place with things already in it" before a word of 15 is spoken. Walk-away #2 is "more exists than the paperwork says", and a grid full of lanterns carries that; a line cannot. Devon's defence of the route (equal segments, no figure, no end marker) removes every explicit progress cue, but a line that is solid for one quarter and dashed for three still reads as a quarter done from the back row, however equal the segments; that is a proportion claim the script never makes. I concede my dashed plots are Mara's dashed plates under another name, and I take her version: plates beside the grid on 13, plates alone on 33. Can I live with Devon's route? Only with the gates dropped from 33 and the three plates kept; I would still argue it is the wrong carrier, but it would not break the deck.

**2. The gate.** These are the same drawing once the bar moves. A hurdle is two posts and a bar; Mara's gate is two green posts and a bar that lifts. So: take 12's hurdle silhouette (already on screen three times), make the posts green per the colour rule, and give the bar the `open` verb the kit already has. Back-row legibility is decided by the states, not the glyph: closed is the bar horizontal at mid-post height; open is the bar swung vertical against the far post, boom-barrier fashion. A bar that merely rises a few pixels is invisible at 60 px from twelve metres. Concession to Devon: the hurdle, not a new shape. Concession to Mara: it moves, and it is green.

**3. The agent glyph.** I concede entirely: the hexagon everywhere. A 24 px hexagon with two dots still reads as the hexagon; a ringed dot reads as a new character the room has to learn on slide 21 for one appearance. Three hexagons inside the fence on 21 is the right miniature of 7.

**4. Rings on 33.** Grey dashed, and I would fight this one. Orange has meant "a person decided" for thirty-one slides. At 1366 projected, a hollow orange ring 20 px across with a 2.5 px stroke and a filled orange dot are indistinguishable past the fourth row; the back half of the room would see six decisions already made, which is the exact overclaim the honesty pass removed. Grey dashed is already the deck's word for "not yet established", and six of them next to six requests need no legend. Devon's instinct (the ask drawn as the missing mark) is right; the colour is wrong. I can live with hollow orange only at 28 px diameter and 3 px stroke, and I do not think that fits beside a 60 px miniature.

**5. Connect the decisions to the gaps, or nothing.** Nothing. Mara is right that the script does not claim the mapping, and there is a second reason: connecting three of six leaves three orphans, and every DCIO will ask what the other three are for. Devon's version connects all six, but to features (a gate, the solid stretch, the yard's gate) that only exist if the route wins dispute 1. Adjacency does the work: plates at the right edge, still dashed, with the same heading they had on 13; the audience draws the line themselves, which is stronger than drawing it for them. I withdraw my round-1 connectors. Can I live with Devon's six connectors? Only if the route wins and the script gains the words to label its features; otherwise they are lines to nowhere.

**6. Evidence labels on stage.** Adopt all three, and they are script changes first. Position: one slot, learned once. 06 has already taught it: a slate caption at 18 px, bottom-left of the drawing field, aligned to the band's left margin. On 04 it sits under the first column, so the source reads before the bars. On 10 it sits under the agent, left of the ledger, which stays at centre-bottom. On 26 it sits below the x-axis label, left of the quadrant. Timing: a caption arrives with the first object of the picture and never last; the last thing to arrive is the point, and "Illustrative" is never the point. Mara's quibble that 10's "Approved" is a new word applies here too: whatever the caption says comes from the You say or it does not go on.

**7. Mara's fixes.** Green-for-staff on 14 and 32: adopt, no timing change, and it is a correctness fix not a taste one. Lighten 27: adopt; lanes as lines, cards as the card, foundations as plain tiles. It removes four fills that each cost a `fill` beat, so 27 gets about 0.3 s back. Drop the plaque cords on 16: adopt, trivial. The 30 levels as four gate settings: adopt the idea, veto the detail. A "drafting slot" and a "stop lever" are 6 px features at the band's 40 px scale and the back row will see four identical hurdles. Four states that do read: no gate at all (people only); bar down with a card waiting on the near side; bar down with the orange mark standing beside the post; bar up with the orange mark beside it. Absent, down, down-plus-person, up-plus-person is a legible ladder. Each gate draws with its band, 0.2 s after it, so 30 lengthens by about 0.8 s and stays well inside its budget.

**8. Density of Devon's 33.** It fits as a document and fails as a frame. At 1366×768 the drawing field after title and band is roughly 1240 × 540. Devon's stack is route with three two-line signposts (about 70 px), three gates (60), connectors (40), six minis (60), rings (24), and two-line request plus two-line explanation (about 90), with gaps: about 420 px tall. It fits numerically. But that is eighteen label words above the road and twenty-four below, in six 200 px columns, with no hero and no breathing room; the eye lands nowhere. Devon's own fallback (drop the gates) still leaves nine labelled objects on the upper line. Two rows of three (each cell about 400 × 230) plus the three plates in a 320 px column at the right edge gives every miniature air, keeps every caption at 18 px, and leaves a 30 px gutter between rows. So the layout decision in dispute 1 and the density decision here are the same decision, and they agree.

One thing to carry forward that is not a dispute: Devon's refinement of 29, where the card stops at staff approval and does not reach the resident because that crossing belongs to 32, is better than my version. Take it.
