# Storyboard room, round 1: Mara Quell

## 1. The story in one paragraph

Two people went away, came back with four lessons, and discovered the lessons were already half-practised at home. That is the spine, and it is quieter and better than a race. The emotional arc runs from humility (we learned this from others; enterprise spent big and stumbled wherever nobody had drawn a line) through recognition (the City has more than its paperwork says: people, a fence, a working fleet, outside applause) to a calm request (draw the remaining lines, name the owners, fund the scope). Every lesson, every failure and every ask in this deck is about the same thing: **a line someone draws, a system that holds it, and a person who stands at it.** PocketOS had no line. Lesson two is the line enforced. The sandbox is a fence. The four levels are four settings of one gate. "Leadership sets the limits" is who draws it. "Keep decisions about residents with accountable staff" is the one line that must hold. The single idea the DCIOs should carry out of the room: *AI works inside lines that people draw, the City already has the people and the fence, and the decisions on the table are the lines still to be drawn.* The reveal on 34 then lands as "you have been watching the lessons applied," and the close asks them to draw.

## 2. The visual system

**Find it in the frames, don't invent it.** The deck already has a cast and three places; the six rebuilt slides are the ones that forgot. Here is the system I would write on the wall.

### Cast (five characters, drawn one way each)

- **The staff figure.** Green, solid-ish, the figure from 9, 11, 23, 24. Green means City people at work. It is wrong in navy on 14 ("A service need") and 32 ("City staff"); fix both. Residents and outside people are navy outline, never green.
- **The agent.** The hexagon from 5, 7, 20, 21. Navy outline, green eyes. Never a server stack, never a sparkle. One shape for every agent in the deck.
- **The card.** The request/document that travels (10, 32, and 6's stream). Navy outline. It is the only thing besides people that moves along a path.
- **The gate.** Two posts and a bar that lifts, drawn once and reused: the permission checker on 7, the permission check on 10, the stage checkpoints on 12, the readiness checks on 21, the levels on 30 (four settings of the same gate), the boundary on 32. Today there are four different gate drawings (box-with-key, two green bars, hurdle glyph, arrow). Pick one. I would take the two green posts from 10 and give them a bar.
- **The marks.** The orange dot is a person deciding (7, 31, 32). The deep-green tick is a check passed (9, 12, 34). Nothing else is orange or deep green. Keep this exactly as the brand doc says; it is the deck's best discipline.

### Places (the City's geography)

- **The grid** of department boxes (15) is the map of the City. 23 should visibly be the same grid, not a different 2×3 arrangement. I would also bring it back, faint, on 13 and 21 so "where the City stands" is literally a place.
- **The fence** (19) is the sandbox. Wherever the sandbox is meant (21 "Azure sandbox", 24 "The Azure team maintains the infrastructure", 33 "Continue the Azure work") draw the fence, small, not a server stack.
- **The table** (22, 23, 24, 25) is where people gather. Round, chairs, that is all. 33 "Authorize the consortium and team" is the table, small.

I am not proposing the first audit's road. A horizontal route with dashed segments ahead is a roadmap cliché, it is one step from the race track you removed, and it adds a fourth geography the audience has not learned. The three positions in walk-away #2 can be shown on the map the deck already has.

### Colour semantics (keep, and police)

Green: City people and process. Indigo: evidence from outside (2, 4, 6, 8, 16, the BNY rule on 11). Navy: words and the structure. Grey: not proposed, missing, failed (8's bands, 22's crossed boxes, 15's "never all in one room" tag). Orange and deep green reserved. Sky/gold/pink on 30 only. Two things to police: the green fills on 27 are the heaviest colour in the deck and say nothing a line would not; and the raised-grey tiles on 9, 12, 31 are fine as stair treads but creep toward card UI.

### Motion vocabulary (verbs that mean something)

- **Ink on** (draw): a thing that exists or is built.
- **Dashed wipe**: a thing proposed or not yet established. Dashed never becomes solid in this deck unless the script says it has been done.
- **Travel** (send/ride): work moving. Only a card or a person travels.
- **Stop**: the limit holds. A travelling thing halts at a gate, with a short still.
- **Mark lands**: a person decided. **Tick lands**: a check passed.
- **Gather**: people converging on a table.
- **Fill**: accumulation (weeks on 19, the notebook on 25, the tray on 6).
- **Half-strength**: not what we propose (22).
- Labels arrive with their object and never move. Nothing bounces. No sweep ever implies a measured gain.

### Composition rules

Title top-left, two lines max. Story band. One drawing, left to right, one hero action per slide. The line is 2.5 px navy; the gate posts are green. Icons at most 36 px and only as the thing a figure holds. No chaining arrows between items that are not a sequence. The last thing to arrive is the point, never a tag.

### Retire

The 14-glyph icon set as a layout device (compass, database, key, eye, magnifier, server, tools-X). The X-as-icon on 14 ("An approved option fits" reads as rejected) and 28 ("Program team" reads as wrong). The bracket and double rule on 21. The chaining arrows on 33. The plaque cords on 16. The hurdle glyph that means "checkpoint" on 21 and "authority levels" on 33 (one glyph, two meanings).

## 4. Risks and what I'd fight for

**Where the pushback will come.** The motion designer will say unifying the gate means redrawing 7, 10, 12, 14, 21, 30, 32, which is seven slides, not six. True, and it is the cheapest change per slide (one part, reused) and the one that makes the system legible. The copywriter will say "Approved" on 10's log and "AI" on 29's journey are new words; they are, and I have flagged both as script changes; if refused, the orange mark alone can carry 10 and 29 can label nothing. The honesty reviewer will worry about 33's empty rings implying leadership has failed to decide; the rings are grey, dashed and empty, which in this deck has only ever meant "not yet established," and the slide title says "can authorize." The engineer will say the grid on 13 and 21 is a carry across worlds, which the handbook forbids; draw it fresh in each world from the same layout constants, which is what 23 should have done anyway.

**The three calls I would defend hardest.**

1. **One gate, drawn one way, from 5 to 32.** The deck's idea is the line and the person at it. Four different gate drawings mean the audience learns it four times and never once. This is the difference between a deck with icons and a deck with a language.
2. **No road.** The map (grid, fence, table) is already three geographies deep and three slides have taught it. A route across 13, 21 and 33 would be a fourth, it would reintroduce the race you removed in spirit if not in label, and it forces a mapping of six decisions onto three gaps that the script does not claim. The dashed plates on 13 returning dashed on 33 is the whole payoff, and it is honest.
3. **33 ends on empty marks, not icons.** The climax of the ask must be recognisable, not new. Six miniatures of slides they have seen, six empty rings in the shape they have watched land all afternoon. That frame is the deck's final argument, and it makes the close a question instead of a summary.

One thing I would not fight for: the 27 lane redraw and the 16 cord removal are taste; do them if there is time after the six.
