## The disputes

**1. The 13→21→33 through-line.** Ines's dashed plots carry walk-away #2 best. The three positions in that walk-away are states of the same three things (unbuilt, still unbuilt while much else turns out to exist, decisions placed beside them), and three dashed shapes that come back dashed say exactly that with no odometer. Devon's route is the better film and the worse argument: a road with a dashed stretch ahead will be read as "how far along" by someone in the third row, however equal the segments, and that is the claim the deck swore off. I concede my grid. I can live with Devon's route only with gates removed from 33 and no figure or sign on it; I would rather not.

**2. The gate.** Hurdle. It is already on 12, 21 and 33, and my "posts with a lifting bar" is a hurdle whose rail lifts. The hurdle must have two drawn states, rail down and rail lifted (the kit's `open`), and one colour, navy, so that the only colour at a gate is the orange mark when a person opens it. 7's permission checker and 10's green bars both become this hurdle. Concede.

**3. The agent glyph.** Hexagon everywhere. Two drawings for one noun is the kind of leak this deck cannot afford, and the hexagon at 24 px in 7's builder row is still a hexagon. If engineering says the builder row needs the ringed dot for density, I can live with it on 7 only, provided 21's fleet miniature and 20's agent are hexagons so that 10, 20 and 21 read as one machine.

**4. The rings on 33.** Grey dashed, with Ines. Grey already means "awaiting" in this deck (22, 15's tag, 30's register lines), and a dashed stroke already means "not yet." A hollow orange ring is a new rule and the brand doc reserves orange for the mark itself; the moment orange appears unfilled, someone will ask whether an unfilled orange elsewhere means something. I concede that hollow orange is the stronger single image. If the brand owner amends the rule in writing, I can live with it.

**5. Connect decisions to the gaps.** I move. The script does make two of the three mappings in its own words: "make the consortium and program team official" against "Dedicated program team," and "agree a funded scope, a budget" against "AI budget line." Draw those two thin lines. The listening tour to "Citywide AI strategy" is an inference (19's You say says listening gives "shared priorities"; 13's plate says "Priorities and measures of success"); draw it only if 33's They see gains one sentence saying the line is drawn. Nothing else connects, no arrows between decisions, the plots stay dashed. I can live with all three lines.

**6. On-stage evidence labels.** Adopt all three, as a small system: a slate caption, same size, same position (foot left, as 06 does it). "BCG, October 2026" on 04. "Illustrative design" on 10 (the You say says it). 26 needs a script line; "Illustrative ideas" is not in 20's entry today, so add it before the world is touched. Four "illustrative" captions in a deck (06, 10, 26, 29) is not wallpaper if they are the same object in the same place; it becomes a signature of honesty, which this audience will notice and repeat.

**7. Ines's legibility points.** Adopt every one.
