# Animation and sequencing audit

2026-10-20 baseline audit, recorded before implementation. The subsequent [clarity and pacing pass](slide-clarity-and-pacing-pass-2026-10-20.md) records the changes now applied.

## Recommendation

Give each slide one clear sequence of attention. Reveal its title, then use either **text → complete visual sequence → still frame** or **complete visual sequence → text → still frame**. Keep the title visible in both patterns. Reveal the two supporting sentences together, as one reading block. Do not return to diagram motion after that block appears in a visual-first slide.

Labels that identify an object belong to the diagram: they may arrive with that object. They should settle quickly and stay still while the object demonstrates its action. A sentence across the top of the slide is different: revealing it redirects attention away from the diagram.

The strongest existing movements should survive: people gathering, a project stopping at a gate, a balance tipping, and work passing through review. Decorative construction should become quieter and quicker. This is primarily a sequencing correction, with a few targeted changes to the visual story.

## Process and evidence

1. Identify the point of each slide and the visual action that explains it.
2. Read every slide's choreography, layouts, and shared motion helpers. Compile all 35 presentation steps to obtain actual start/end times, including staggered text rows.
3. Map the audience's likely attention path: title, supporting text, diagram regions, and final takeaway. Flag reading/motion overlap, reversals, competing regions, and new events after an apparent conclusion.
4. Compare those schedules with the previously inspected final layouts. Check ten representative complex sequences with normal-clock browser captures (display steps 3, 7, 12, 17, 21, 27, 29, 32, 33, and 34). Accelerated browser-clock captures were discarded because they did not reproduce the GSAP clock correctly.
5. Assign a preferred pattern and concrete visual changes to every slide. Establish implementation and acceptance checks before editing.

The audit covers all 34 slide pictures, including both steps of the community slide. The footer/navigation numbers run from 1 to 35; those are the primary numbers below. The script's older identifiers are included to prevent ambiguity. Findings about overlap are measured from compiled schedules. Judgments about attention and clarity are editorial assessments, not eye-tracking results. Browser checkpoints are not a frame-pacing benchmark.

## Findings across the deck

- **Every content slide has at least one supporting sentence whose reveal overlaps diagram motion.** Most have both. The overlap is intentional in the current code: `with: true` starts a sentence during the preceding visual beat.
- **Titles also compete with early drawings.** Several worlds begin drawing while the new title is still arriving. Separate the title's arrival from the first substantive visual action.
- **Text is generally rising from a mask, not literally typing character by character.** The attention problem remains: a new sentence becomes readable somewhere else while the diagram is moving.
- **The current 12-second build cap and 3-second maximum still interval work against reading time.** Preserve a bounded duration, but replace these blanket constraints with explicit per-slide phase budgets. Do not meet an old cap by making the audience read faster.
- **Repeated staggered arrivals create too many events.** People, chairs, names, captions, ticks, and borders often arrive separately even when they convey one idea.
- **Some diagrams finish on secondary information.** A late statistic, small tag, or extra caption steals emphasis from the main action.
- **Several existing tests require the problematic overlap.** Those assertions must be replaced with tests of the new phase boundaries, while keeping checks for final state, meaningful action order, geometry, and navigation.

## Proposed motion rules

1. **One main focus at a time.** Related parts of one action may move together; unrelated regions should not start competing actions.
2. **One supporting-text reveal per slide.** Both sentences appear together, with no stagger between them. Start with a short fade or very small rise, approximately 0.25–0.4 seconds. Keep the text fixed afterward.
3. **Title first.** Use a short, consistent arrival, approximately 0.4–0.6 seconds, and let it settle before the next phase. Avoid a prolonged reveal line by line.
4. **Allow actual reading time.** For text-first slides, use a provisional reading allowance based on the word count: approximately `words / 3.5` seconds, with a three-second minimum, then tune during rehearsal. This is a starting heuristic, not a claim about every reader. Long blocks can require 8–12 seconds. Visual-first slides can leave the final text on screen until the presenter advances.
5. **Pause at meaning, not every object.** A clear approval, arrival, or comparison deserves roughly 0.4–0.7 seconds of stillness. A chair or border usually does not.
6. **Use motion to show relationships.** Draw a connector toward its destination; move a person from one role to another; stop a work item at the actual decision boundary. Keep labels near their subjects.
7. **Use consistent easing without making everything identical.** Short fades for labels; smooth deceleration for arrivals; no bounce or overshoot on evidence or charts; continuous speed through joined path segments. Keep coordinated objects on the same timing curve. Exact durations need normal-speed review.
8. **Finish decisively.** Once the takeaway is visible, nothing else starts. No decorative looping. Preserve the stable final frame and the existing ability to finish a build with another press.
9. **Keep meaningful reading on stationary text.** Replace moving quote text and rapid serial text reveals with stable blocks and restrained emphasis.

## First implementation pass: three examples

Use these to establish the pacing before applying the rule everywhere. The timings are proposed targets for rehearsal, not tested replacements.

**Slide 29, outcomes — visual first.** Approximately 0–0.6 s title; 0.6–1.5 s title hold; 1.5–3 s strategic context and project dots; 3–4.5 s activity tally; 4.5–5 s deliberate pause; 5–7 s replace it with baseline, target, and responsibility; 7–7.5 s settle; 7.5–7.9 s reveal both sentences. Then hold indefinitely. No new motion after the sentences.

**Slide 12, manageable work — text first.** Title → supporting block → word-count-based reading hold → criteria appear as one quiet group → stair and project establish → first stage and check → second stage and stop. The criteria are stable while the project moves. Budget the reading and movement separately; do not force the whole slide back under 12 seconds.

**Slide 32, residents — visual first.** Establish the people and the boundary → AI prepares the card → card waits by staff → approval → card crosses → resident question → both supporting sentences. Give the approval a short distinct pause. This tests whether the motion communicates the intended rule even without narration.

## Implementation order

1. **Define the ATS sequencing contract.** Add explicit title, reading, diagram, and concluding-text phases in the ATS choreography layer. Keep timing declared in the source so playback and direct jumps share the same final state. Prefer an ATS helper over changing shared engine behavior. Update the relevant documentation and replace overlap requirements in tests.
2. **Implement and review the three examples above.** Preserve their final information. Review in normal time at 1920×1080 and 1366×768 with the speaker notes. Set the reading-time defaults from those examples.
3. **Apply the common phase separation to every slide.** Move both story sentences into the selected phase; stop title/diagram overlap; group labels and remove redundant ornamental staggers. Preserve the existing 35-step navigation, including the community slide's second click.
4. **Revise the most demanding visual sequences.** Work in this order: community (17–18), lanes (27), five decisions (33), fleet (7), existing work (15), current City position (21), listening (25), idea assessment (26), skills (28), and the four-lesson reveal (34). Then complete the remaining table entries.
5. **Synchronize the script.** Update each `They see` description and add concise delivery cues inside the existing `You say` paragraph where useful. Keep the approved spoken meaning. Regenerate the six script modules; never maintain a separate handwritten app version. Do not put unsupported extra note fields in the markdown parser.
6. **Validate and rehearse.** Run structural and motion-contract tests, typecheck, build, and browser checks. Review normal-speed recordings for every slide; still screenshots alone cannot approve animation pacing. Rehearse the complete talk to update its timing estimate.

## Files and acceptance checks

- Per-slide edits belong in the `steps-*.ts` file matching each slide ID, under `src/talks/ats/worlds/{world}/`. Geometry changes for residents, five decisions, and possibly level registration belong in their corresponding `layout-*.ts` and scene files.
- Shared phase policy belongs in `src/talks/ats/worlds/steps.ts` or a focused adjacent ATS helper, used by each world's `play` wrapper. Avoid a global replacement of `with: true`: many overlaps coordinate parts of one useful action.
- Test that the title has settled before the main phase; text-first diagrams start after their reading window; visual-first text starts after all diagram actions finish; both supporting sentences share one reveal; no new movement follows the final text. Allow only the documented community two-step exception.
- Retain action-specific checks: approval before crossing; review before final approval; project checks before advancing; milestone/path geometry; no objects or captions leaving the slide; stable finished picture.
- Replace the global 12-second and 3-second-still expectations with explicit ATS phase budgets and a bounded overall allowance. Start with a 24-second safety ceiling for text-first builds, with any longer case requiring simplification or a deliberate exception. Do not silently stretch every diagram to fill that allowance.
- Update the browser check's fixed `LONGEST_BUILD = 12500` when longer builds are introduced. Prefer deriving the interruption wait from the declared maximum plus a margin, so it still catches late movement after a cut.
- Preserve backward navigation, jump-to-slide, direct URLs, finish-on-second-press, and the notes controls. Compare final screenshots reached by playback, backward cut, and direct load.
- Review the reading window and local diagram-label density with narration. A timing test can prevent overlap, but cannot prove that an audience has understood a dense diagram.

The end state should feel calmer and more deliberate while preserving the deck's visual identity: read once, follow one complete visual explanation, and arrive at a clear stopping point.
