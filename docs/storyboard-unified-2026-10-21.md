# Unified storyboard and visual system

2026-10-21. This is a synthesis of a two-round storyboard session among three invited creative personas, working from the script, all 35 finished frames ([storyboard-frames/](storyboard-frames/)) and the earlier audits:

- **Mara Quell**, brand-system creative director (in the mould of Collins). Her focus was the underlying idea, a disciplined symbol set and restraint.
- **Devon Ashby**, story strategist trained in the Duarte school, who shapes Gartner-style research briefings. Their focus was the audience's journey, the sparkline, and evidence labelled for what it is.
- **Ines Varga**, design director at an executive-briefing multimedia studio. Her focus was choreography in the room, legibility from the back row, and timing.

In round 1 each persona storyboarded the whole deck independently. In round 2 each read the other two memos, conceded or held each disputed point, and revised the final frames for slides 13, 21 and 33. Their memos are in [storyboard-room/](storyboard-room/). This document records where the room landed. Where they split, it gives the majority call and names the dissent, and it leaves the owner's decisions to you. Nothing here has been built, and no script has been changed.

---

## 1. The story

Two of the City's own people went away, came back with four plain lessons, and then held those lessons up against the City. They found it already practising much of them, unorganized and uncounted.

The arc is a **correction of self-image**:
- **Humility:** enterprise spent big and stumbled wherever nobody drew a line.
- **The lessons:** taught by things stopping at gates and people choosing the smaller tool.
- **Recognition:** the City has more than its paperwork says.
- **A deliberately modest plan:** meet, form a team, listen, sort, deliver, train, measure.
- **The pen handed to the room:** leadership draws the lines, each DCIO sets autonomy, the CISO owns the controls, and six decisions wait.

**What every slide is about:** a line someone draws, a system that holds it, and a person who stands at it. PocketOS had no line. Lesson two is the line enforced. The sandbox is a fence. The four levels are settings of one limit. Serving residents is the one line that must hold.

**The single idea to leave behind:** *the work is already here; what is missing is organization and six decisions that are yours to make.* Every visual choice should make "already here" tangible and make "decision" visibly unmade.

### Sparkline (Devon, endorsed by the room)

```
COULD BE    03 lessons     09–12 lessons taught    15–21 what the City really has    23–29 the plan     34 reveal
             /     \          /           \           /                     \            /        \       /
WHAT IS  01–02     04–08 enterprise         13–14 on paper,                   22 only        30–33 ready,   35
         course    stumbles                 no program                        meetings       the ask        talk
```

Part 4 is the long climb the DCIOs must feel. The dip at 22 is deliberate, because modesty makes the ask credible. Slide 33 is held open, and slide 34 closes it.

## 2. The visual system (the deck's bible)

The system is found in the frames, not invented. The deck already has a cast and places. The six rebuilt slides are the ones that forgot them.

### Cast: the only things that move

| Character | Drawing | Meaning | Where today | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Staff figure** | Green outline person, drawn at cast scale (60–80 px at 1366) | City people at work. It is the only thing that climbs, gathers or approves. | 9, 11, 23, 24 | Navy by mistake on 14 and 32; tiny glyphs on 13, 21, 28, 29 |
| **Person / resident** | The same figure in navy | Residents and outside people | 32 | Keep staff and residents distinguished by colour, not shape |
| **Agent** | Hexagon with two dots, at two sizes (alone, and a 24 px row glyph) | An agent | 5, 10, 20, 21 | Unanimous: the hexagon everywhere. Slide 7's ringed dots become hexagons. |

| **Gate** | 12's hurdle silhouette with **green posts**. Two states: bar horizontal (closed) and bar swung vertical (open, boom-barrier style), using the kit's `open` | A limit enforced | Four different drawings today (7's box with a key, 10's two bars, the 12/21 hurdle, 33's hurdle-as-icon) | One drawing on 7, 10, 12, 14, 20, 21, 29, 32. A bar that only lifts a few pixels is invisible at the back, so it must swing. |

### Places

**The grid, the fence and the table.** These are three geographies the audience has already learned. Add no route or road; all three personas ended against it, because a part-solid, part-dashed line reads as "a quarter done" from the back row, which is the same claim the 2026-10-20 pass removed.

### Colour semantics (write it down and police it)

- **Green:** City people and process.
- **Indigo:** outside evidence (the course, Gartner, BCG, recognition, the BNY rule).
- **Navy:** words and structure.
- **Grey:** not proposed, failed, or awaiting.
- **Dashed:** not yet established. It never becomes solid unless the script says the thing has been done.
- **Orange and deep green:** reserved for the marks.
- **Sky, gold, pink:** the levels on slide 30 only.

### Motion vocabulary: each verb means one thing

| Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Draw on** | Something exists or is established |
| **Dashed wipe** | Proposed or not yet established |
| **Travel** | Work moving toward a decision point. Constant speed, with ease-out on arrival only. |
| **Stop / wait** | The limit holds. Give it 0.4–0.7 s of stillness. This is the most important verb in the deck. |
| **Open** | A person let it through, and the orange mark lands first |
| **Tick lands** | A check passed. Never used on illustrations or criteria. |
| **Gather** | People converging on the table |
| **Fill** | Accumulation (weeks, the notebook, the tray) |
| **Half-strength** | Not what we propose |

Labels arrive with their object and stay still while it acts. Nothing sweeps, bounces or speeds up. **Each slide has one hero verb.**

### Composition and legibility rules (add to the build handbook)

1. Title top-left, two lines at most, green rule; story band; then one drawing field.
2. **The subject fills the drawing field's height.** The six rebuilt slides use about a third of it.
3. **Nothing under 18 px at 1366.** Slide 33's current captions are about 17 px.
4. Left-to-right is time and top-to-bottom is hierarchy; never mix them on one slide. Slide 31 is the one legitimate top-down slide.
5. No arrows between items that are not a sequence.
6. Equal size on a shared baseline wherever the script says "not a ranking".
7. More than about nine labels means either two slides or an honest list (as slide 8 is).
8. The last thing to arrive is the point, never a tag or a caption.

### The honesty slot

One device, learned once from slide 06: a **slate caption at 18 px, bottom-left of the drawing field**, arriving with the first object, never last. Use it wherever a slide shows a source, a forecast or an illustration. Proposed uses: 04 "BCG, October 2026"; 06 (existing); 10 "Illustrative design"; 26 illustrative ideas; 29 (its project tag already does this job). The room judged that the same object in the same place becomes a signature of credibility that this audience will notice and repeat.

### Retire

- The 14-glyph `Sketch` icon set as a layout device: compass, cylinder, key, eye, magnifier, server, and the tools-X, which reads as "wrong" on 14 and 28.
- Glyph + bold label + caption rows.
- The bracket and double rule on 21, and the double rule on 20.
- The chaining arrows on 33.
- The scroll on 13.
- The cylinder standing in for a budget, which reads as a database beside a data key.
- 16's plaque cords.
- 9's solid "T", which looks like a logo; make it an outline that fills, as the script says.
- Calendar cells and chairs arriving one at a time.

## 3. Storyboard, all 35 steps

Format: **the point** · what the audience sees happen · → the hand-off to the next slide. ★ marks the focus slides. For those, beats are in seconds after the reading pause (text-first slides: title, both sentences, a 0.6 s pause, then the visual).

**01 — Title.** Who, what, for whom. Navy; title, rule and both lines settle; still. → The room settles.

**02 — The course.** It was hands-on. Four indigo tiles land as units; four green benches draw as one row. This is the colour lesson begins: indigo is what we were taught, green is what we did. → The green numerals on 03.

**03 — Four lessons.** Count them, so you recognise them later. Four panels in one pass; the thin three-stop agenda line as one quiet unit. That line is the deck's agenda and the only route the deck has. → "Enterprise now".

**04 — Enterprise is spending.** Money moved, mostly to "boost". Two columns rise, then the split bar, with the middle filling green (City staff knowledge matters here). *Add* the honesty slot "BCG, October 2026", the deck's only evidence slide with no source shown. → Green "boost" seeds lesson three.

**05 — Chatbot to agent.** An agent loops and acts, so it needs limits. Timeline, then the hexagon is introduced, the five-stop loop lights **once**, and the "Stops when permission is needed" bar closes it. Draw that bar as the gate's bar and move it clear of "Goal". It sets the one-lap rule for 28. → The first gate.

**06 — Human oversight has to scale.** The system flags; people take the exceptions. Cards stream through the checks and the exceptions drop to the tray; the person with the orange mark stands between the trays, and orange means "a person decides" from here on. Give "Human oversight" its own column (it currently reads as part of "Audit samples"). Keep the "Illustrative flow" caption. → The approver on 07.

**07 — A fleet.** Separate roles, a gate above the builders, a person at the end. The groups appear left to right, and the builders become hexagons. Redraw the permission checker as **the gate**, which introduces it here. "No agent grades its own work" draws as a line. → The room now knows gate, line and mark.

**08 — Failures.** Each failure was a missing limit. The indigo figure, three cards with grey bands, grey tags, then one line draws under all three: "No one had limited what the AI could do." Give that line half a second of stillness, because it is the hinge of the deck. → The four lessons are how you draw it.

**09 — Lesson one.** Stop at the first step that works. The stair rises; the green figure climbs onto the first tread and **stops**; 0.5 s still; tick and "Stop here if it gets the job done"; the grey oversight arrow; the plain tool fills in outline. This is the deck's best motion and the model for every rebuild: a character travels, stops, and a mark lands. → The figure who stopped is the person who will stand at the gate.

**★ 10 — Lesson two: enforce the agent's limits.** *Point:* a limit written in instructions is a wish; a limit built into the system is a wall; and a log says what happened. Three controls, one machine.
- 0.0–0.6: the hexagon agent lands at the left (cast scale). The gate (closed) draws at centre with "Permission check". The record stands beyond it. An empty ledger draws under the gate with "Keep an action log". The honesty slot, "Illustrative design", sits under the agent.
- 0.6–1.7: card "Requested action / Delete a record" travels and **stops hard** at the bar. 0.5 s still.
- 2.2–2.6: "Blocked" stamps onto the card itself; "Deletion is not allowed" appears at the gate; the record does not flinch, with "The record stays intact" beside it. The first ledger line draws as an unlabelled stroke, as in 25's notebook.
- 3.0–4.0: a second card, "A different action needs approval", travels to the gate and **waits**. 0.5 s.
- 4.5–5.5: the green staff figure steps to the post; the orange mark lands ("Wait for a person to authorize it"); the gate **opens**; the card passes. The second ledger line draws, with "Record requests, approvals, and outcomes" underneath.
- *Final frame (about 5.8 s):* agent; gate open, with one card blocked in front of it and one through; the person and mark at the post; the record intact; a two-line ledger. No new label words.
- → To 11: the person at the gate is the trained staff member lesson three is about.

**11 — Lesson three.** Train the people who know the service. Four stages left to right; arrows are honest here because it is a real sequence; one of two products is circled; the BNY example is set apart behind its indigo rule. Keep. Its screen, checklist and circled product become props on 28. → Trained people judge where to start.

**12 — Lesson four.** Start where mistakes are cheap, with a check at each stage. Criteria settle as one still, unfilled group; a stair with four **gates** (the one drawing); the green project box climbs to Explore, tick; to Validate, tick; stops. Deploy and Scale stay closed. → To 13: "how does the City compare". The empty treads become the City's empty plates.

At 2.2, 2.8 and 3.4, three dashed grey plates wipe in on the right, labels inside with room around them and captions clear of the dashes: "Citywide AI strategy / Priorities and measures of success", "Dedicated program team / Responsibility and staff capacity", "AI budget line / Resources for agreed priorities". ## 5. Open decisions for you

**A. Should lines on 33 connect decisions to the gaps from 13?**
- *Two lines* (Mara, Devon): "Authorize the consortium and team" to "Dedicated program team", and "Allocate funding" to "AI budget line". These are word-for-word matches a cold reader can check; they need one added sentence in 33's *They see*.
- *No lines* (Ines): connecting two of six leaves four orphans, and every DCIO will ask what the others are for. Placing them side by side lets the room make the connection itself.
- My recommendation is **no lines**. Placing them side by side already makes the point, and it avoids the orphan question.

**B. Should slide 30's levels be drawn as four settings of the gate?** Ines's legible version uses four states: no gate (people only); bar down with a card waiting; bar down with the mark beside the post; bar up with the mark. That adds about 0.8 s. Mara wants this. Devon vetoed it for this edition: it gives the gate another meaning on the slide the room must memorize, and "No-go" drawn as an absence is a negative. My recommendation is **defer to a second edition**. Slide 30 already works, and the risk sits on the slide that matters most.

**C. Gate post colour:** green (majority, and matching 10 today) or navy (Mara). My recommendation is green.
